Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026 · Contact: toliv.support@gmail.com

In short: your data is seen only by your squad (at most 15 friends). Your weight and body stats are private. Health data is never sold, used for ads, or shared for marketing. You can delete your account at any time.

1. Who we are

toliv ("the app", formerly Healthy Together) is an iPhone app run by Inderpreet Singh, based in Toronto, Canada. It's currently in a friends-only beta.

Person responsible for your personal information (privacy officer): Inderpreet Singh, owner. Contact: toliv.support@gmail.com.

3. Apple Health

We read steps, workouts and active energy only with your permission, which you can change at any time in the Health app. We don't write to Apple Health. Health data is never used for advertising, sold, or shared with data brokers or marketers. Steps or workouts you typed in by hand are shown but don't earn points. When Apple Health records a workout of 10 minutes or more, the app posts its type, length and calories to your squad's feed; workouts you typed into Health by hand are not posted. Workouts you log with a photo or clip are never written to Apple Health.

4. AI food recognition and food lookups

When you use the AI estimate, the meal photo is sent to the Google Gemini API to identify the foods and portions. During the beta we use Gemini's free tier, under which Google may use submitted photos to improve its products. We'll move to Google's paid tier, which doesn't allow that, and update this policy when we do.

Meals logged from search, a saved meal, Ask AI or typed in still have their photo sent to Gemini to check it shows food. Only meals logged without a photo (up to 4 a week) send nothing. With that check, the names of the foods you logged are sent too, to see that the photo roughly matches; your squad sees a note if it doesn't.

If you tap Ask AI in food search, only the text you typed (e.g. "medium iced capp") is sent to Gemini to estimate it. When you log a workout with a photo or 5-second clip (no sound), that photo or clip, the details you typed and your current weight are sent to Gemini to check it looks like a workout and estimate the calories.

Calories and macros come from our own nutrition table. When a food isn't in it, only the food's name (never your photo or anything about you) is looked up in the public USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts databases. If you scan a barcode, only the barcode number is looked up in Open Food Facts. Indian recipe values come from the Indian Nutrient Databank, stored on our server.

A photo taken in Label or Barcode mode is your meal photo: it's kept and your squad sees it, like any meal photo. If you scan an extra label from the review screen, that photo is sent to Gemini to read the numbers and kept privately; it's never shown to your squad.

Most meals are logged with a photo taken in the app. You can log up to 4 meals a week without a photo, and any meal picked from search or your saved meals while you have no squad-mates. If you're in a squad, it sees a "No photo" note on those meals.

Your usual portions. When you change the AI's portion of the same food the same way several times, your next scan of that food starts at your usual portion, says so, and offers Undo. This is learned only from your own edits, is private to you, and only changes your own estimates.

Your usual items. At a restaurant chain, a scan that can't tell which menu item it is may pick the item you usually log there; it says "Your usual" and offers Undo. This uses only your own meals from the last 90 days, is private to you, and only changes your own estimates.

Improving food recognition (opt-in). With Help improve food recognition switched on (Settings → Your photos; off by default), your meal photos and corrections may be used to improve our own food recognition, and your corrected numbers for a food (never your photo, name or anything else about you) may count toward a shared correction that improves everyone's estimate of it. A shared correction needs several people from different squads to agree, and is applied only after the developer approves it; it shows as "Corrected by N people" with Undo. To make sure each photo is named correctly, someone on our team may look at the photo and check the food's name. After you've corrected a couple of AI estimates, the app asks you once whether to switch this on; nothing changes unless you say yes. You can switch it off at any time; photos and corrections from while it was off are never used. Switching it off also deletes the checked names for your photos.

5. Sign-in and notifications

If you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive your name and email address from them (Apple may give us a private relay address instead). If you turn on notifications, we store your device's push token (deleted when you sign out) and send notifications through Apple's push service as things happen (race alerts and jabs never between 9 pm and 8 am). Each type can be switched off in the app.

If you pin the race to your Lock Screen (Live Activity), it shows your place and one squad-mate's name on your phone only, is never sent anywhere, and ends when you sign out. You can sit out the race (Profile): no lane, no rival and no race alerts; you stay in the squad quest.

6. Beta website and Android waiting list

The beta invite page (toliv.pages.dev/beta) is hosted by Cloudflare. It has no cookies, ads or analytics. It remembers which join steps you've finished only in your own browser, and its fonts come from Google Fonts, so Google sees that your browser loaded them.

If you join the Android waiting list, we keep only your email address and the date you added it, in a Cloudflare database. We use it for one thing: to email you when toliv is on Android. It's never shared, sold or used for anything else. To be taken off the list, email toliv.support@gmail.com.

7. Where data is stored

On Supabase (database, file storage and server functions), hosted in Canada (AWS ca-central-1). Photos are kept in private storage and shown through links that expire after an hour.

Some data leaves Canada. Photos, clips and text sent for an AI estimate or check are processed by Google (Gemini API) in the United States. If you sign in with Apple or Google, they handle your sign-in, and notifications go through Apple's push service. The Android waiting list is stored by Cloudflare in eastern North America, which may be the United States. Data processed outside Canada is covered by the laws of that country, and its courts and authorities may be able to access it.

8. How long we keep it

While your account is active. This includes every photo you take in the app, also ones you retake, don't log, or whose meal you delete; those are private to you and never shown to your squad. If you delete your account, your photos and app usage are deleted immediately and everything else 7 days later, in case you change your mind. App usage is kept for 180 days, then only as daily totals with no name attached. Android waiting list emails are deleted once we've sent the "toliv is on Android" email, or sooner if you ask.

9. Your choices and rights

  • See and correct your data in the app (Profile and Settings).
  • Hide calorie numbers from yourself, or hide your progress from your squad.
  • Leave your squad at any time.
  • Delete your account: Profile → Your data → Delete account.
  • Export a copy of your data any time in Settings → Your data, ask us to correct it, or ask any question: toliv.support@gmail.com. We answer within 30 days.
  • Withdraw your consent at any time: turn off Apple Health access, notifications or Share app usage, log meals without the AI estimate, or delete your account.
  • You can also complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or, in Quebec, the Commission d'accès à l'information.

10. Security and breaches

Access to your data is limited to your squad by database rules, and photos are only reachable through short-lived links. If a breach of your data creates a real risk of significant harm, we'll tell you and the Privacy Commissioner as soon as we can, and keep a record of every breach.

11. Children

The app is for people 14 and over. We ask for your birth year when you sign up and don't let younger people create an account. If we learn that someone under 14 has an account, we'll delete it.

12. Changes

We'll tell you in the app before any change that affects how your data is used.
